# Addis Eats: Design Context

Source of truth for every visual decision in this project. If a change cannot be
justified against this file, it does not belong in the UI.

## Users

**Hungry people in Addis Ababa, deciding what to eat, on a phone.**

They are not shopping. They already know roughly what they want and they want
it soon. The decision is fast and emotional: does this look good, how much is
it, how long will it take. They will abandon the whole thing if the menu is slow
or the prices are hard to scan.

There is a second audience: **kitchen and counter staff** working a queue of
incoming orders on a screen in a hot room, glancing at it between other tasks.
They need to read the next order at arm's length.

The job to be done is *appetite plus certainty*: this looks good, and I know
what it costs and when it arrives.

## Brand Personality

**Appetising. Direct. Warm without being cute.**

The voice is a good waiter: knows the menu, tells you what is actually good
today, does not oversell, does not use the word "delicious" about its own food.
Short sentences. Real descriptions of the dish.

Emotional goal: hunger and confidence. The interface should make the food look
like food and then get out of the way.

## Aesthetic Direction

**Fresh market green, cut with lime and orange.** Deep forest green is the
brand's own colour and the ground the page opens and closes on; lime is the one
thing on screen that looks pressable; orange is the warmth that keeps the green
from reading as a hospital. Cream paper instead of white.

The reference objects are a produce market and a modern grocery brand, not a
printed technical document and not a spice market. Two directions were replaced
to get here: "Clinical Swiss" (zero radius, hairlines, mono data) read as an
instrument panel, and the intermediate "Bold and appetising" pass (berbere red,
saffron, Fraunces) was warm but fought the client's teal logo and read as a
restaurant rather than a delivery brand.

### Concrete commitments

| Dimension | Decision |
|---|---|
| **Type** | Archivo for everything structural, set **heavy and uppercase** in headlines, tight tracking, to give the page its shout. Caveat carries one orange script subline under each section heading. Mono survives only for order numbers and timers, never for prices. |
| **Colour** | Forest green is the signal (brand, primary ink, section indices). **Lime is the accent** and fills every primary control, always with dark green type on it. **Orange is the highlight**, used only for the script sublines and small emphases, never for a control and never for body copy. Cream paper, green hairlines. |
| **Primary action** | Lime with dark green type, pressing to deep green with cream. On the dark green field it stays lime. |
| **Depth** | Green-tinted soft shadows. Cards lift on hover; the closing band sits flat. |
| **Radius** | Generous and consistent: 8px small, 12px medium, 20px large, 28px extra large. |
| **Rhythm** | Four sections in three different shapes: an asymmetric split, a full-width grid, the split again carrying an ordered list, then a single filled slab. No two consecutive sections are built the same way. |
| **Food photography** | Large and untreated inside cards, with real radius. The photograph is the appetite; inside a card nothing covers it. |
| **Motion** | State changes plus gentle hover lifts. Exponential ease-out. Nothing bounces. |
| **Icons** | Hand-written inline SVG at a single stroke weight. Never emoji. |

### The colour rules that keep this system honest

The brand is green, so green can no longer mean anything. Three separations
carry the whole palette:

- **`--ok` is teal, not green.** A green "confirmed" tick on a green panel says
  nothing. Teal sits far enough away to still read as a distinct state.
- **`--highlight` is orange and is never a control.** It cannot reach body-copy
  contrast on either surface, so it carries large script type only. Measured at
  4.74:1 on paper and 4.48:1 on the green field, both clear of the 3:1 floor
  that large type owes.
- **`--accent-ink` and `--signal-strong` are fixed in both schemes.** The lime
  fill stays light in both, and the pressed green stays deep in both, so the
  type on each has to stay put as well. Tokens that sit on the *page* use
  `--signal-ink`, which is the one that flips.

### Copy rules

No em dashes or en dashes in user-facing text. They are an AI tell. Use commas,
full stops, or restructure the sentence. Code comments are exempt.

### The hero lockup

The home hero is a **full-bleed photograph with type over it**, which is the
reference's composition:

1. A photograph fills the band edge to edge, breaking out of the page container
   on both sides.
2. A green scrim runs across it on a 100deg axis, so the type sits on a colour
   the system controls rather than on whatever the photograph is doing.
3. The copy column is capped at `40rem`: an orange Caveat script line, a heavy
   uppercase headline, one sentence of lede, two buttons, and a one-line trust
   row. The trust row replaced an earlier four-cell stat strip.
4. A lime disc sits in the far corner above 960px, carrying one short line. It
   is dropped below that width, where it would collide with the headline.

Two rules worth keeping: the copy column is sized in `rem`, never in `ch`,
because `ch` resolves against the element's own 16px font and silently clamps
the headline to about 410px, which pushes the call to action below the fold.
And the breakout needs `overflow-x: clip` on the root, not `hidden`, because
`hidden` creates a scroll container and breaks the sticky header.



### Anti-references

- **The previous version of this app.** A dark card (`#181b20`) with a 28px
  radius, a vertical gradient fading the dish photo into the card body, frosted
  `backdrop-filter: blur(12px)` badges on top of the image, and pill buttons.
  Every one of those is a recognisable template move.
- **A delivery marketplace.** Neon accents, dark mode by default, glowing
  badges, countdown pressure.
- **A "rustic artisan" brand.** A high-contrast display serif, a kraft-paper
  texture, and the word "handcrafted". Cream is a paper colour here, not a
  costume: it is a flat surface with green hairlines on it.
- **Brutalism for its own sake.** Sharp corners are a consequence of the
  printed-menu metaphor, not a style in search of a justification.

### What makes an interface read as AI-generated

The problem is never ugliness. It is *undifferentiation*. AI output is the
statistical average of everything in its training data, so it lands on the same
safe choices every time. The specific tells:

1. The default font stack: here, a display serif paired with a geometric sans,
   which is the standard "make it look premium" reflex.
2. Frosted glass over a photograph.
3. Emoji standing in for icons.
4. Every container being the same rounded, softly shadowed card.
5. A hero stat block: four big numbers in a grid.
6. An uppercase eyebrow above every single section heading.
7. The identical rhythm repeated down the page.
8. Copy with no point of view: true, grammatical, and interchangeable.

The fix is not to add more. It is to make specific choices and let them
constrain everything else.

## Design Principles

1. **Every colour must earn its place.** Lime appears on things the user can
   press, and nowhere else. Orange appears on script sublines, and nowhere
   else. If a colour is doing two jobs, one of them is wrong.
2. **Rules, not boxes.** A hairline separates without adding weight. A card
   around every dish is a decision not to decide.
3. **The food is the decoration.** Photography is the one place this interface
   is allowed to be loud. Everything else stays quiet so it can be.
4. **Numbers align.** Anything a user compares, prices, quantities, order
   numbers, delivery times, sits in tabular figures so it reads as a column.
   Prices are set in the sans, not the mono: the mono is for identifiers.
5. **Type goes on the photograph once, in the hero.** Everywhere else the dish
   photograph stays uncovered. The hero is the exception because a full-bleed
   band needs a ground the system controls, and the scrim provides it.
6. **State is stated, never implied.** Never rely on colour alone to carry
   meaning. Every status has a word next to it.
7. **Accessibility is structural.** 44px targets, 4.5:1 body contrast, visible
   focus, real buttons and forms. Not a checklist applied at the end.
