# Addis Eats — Design Context

Source of truth for every visual decision in this project. If a change cannot be
justified against this file, it does not belong in the UI.

## Users

**Hungry people in Addis Ababa, deciding what to eat, on a phone.**

They are not shopping — they already know roughly what they want and they want
it soon. The decision is fast and emotional: does this look good, how much is
it, how long will it take. They will abandon the whole thing if the menu is slow
or the prices are hard to scan.

There is a second audience: **kitchen and counter staff** working a queue of
incoming orders on a screen in a hot room, glancing at it between other tasks.
They need to read the next order at arm's length.

The job to be done is *appetite plus certainty*: this looks good, and I know
what it costs and when it arrives.

## Brand Personality

**Appetising. Direct. Warm without being cute.**

The voice is a good waiter: knows the menu, tells you what is actually good
today, does not oversell, does not use the word "delicious" about its own food.
Short sentences. Real descriptions of the dish.

Emotional goal: hunger and confidence. The interface should make the food look
like food and then get out of the way.

## Aesthetic Direction

**Bold and appetising.** Saturated, warm, and unafraid of colour. The berbere
hero field is the signature: one deep red surface per page that says "food"
before a single word is read.

The reference objects are a spice market and a restaurant chalkboard menu, not
a printed technical document. The earlier "Clinical Swiss" direction (zero
radius, hairlines, mono data) was replaced because it read as an instrument
panel rather than a kitchen: correct, disciplined, and not the least bit
hungry.

### Concrete commitments

| Dimension | Decision |
|---|---|
| **Type** | Fraunces (display serif, warm and characterful) for headlines and dish names. Archivo for body and UI. Mono survives only for order numbers and timers, never for prices. |
| **Colour** | Warm paper neutral ramp. **Berbere red is the primary signal** (actions, active states); **saffron amber is the secondary accent** (highlights, eyebrow labels). Two warm hues, never cool ones. |
| **Primary action** | Berbere. On the hero field it inverts to cream-on-berbere. |
| **Depth** | Warm-tinted soft shadows. Cards lift on hover; the hero field casts a real shadow. |
| **Radius** | Generous and consistent: 8px small, 12px medium, 16px large, 20px the hero field. Rounded corners make photography feel approachable. |
| **Rhythm** | Sections numbered in saffron uppercase, separated by whitespace more than rules. |
| **Food photography** | Large, square-ish, untreated, with real radius. The photograph is the appetite; covering it up is self-defeating. |
| **Motion** | State changes plus gentle hover lifts. Exponential ease-out. Nothing bounces. |
| **Icons** | Hand-written inline SVG at a single stroke weight. Never emoji. |

### Copy rules

No em-dashes or en-dashes in user-facing text. They are an AI tell. Use commas,
full stops, or restructure the sentence. Code comments are exempt.

### The hero lockup

The home hero is a four-part composition, adapted from a reference the client
supplied:

1. **Left**: eyebrow, the display headline, one sentence of lede, two pill
   buttons, and a one-line trust row (dish count, kitchen sections, delivery).
   The trust row replaces an earlier four-cell stat strip; the same facts, read
   as a sentence rather than as a dashboard.
2. **Right**: one large photograph, portrait 3:4, rounded, casting a real shadow.
3. **Above the photograph**: the wordmark set oversized in Fraunces. It sits
   whole, not as a watermark, so every letter stays legible.
4. **Over the photograph's bottom corner**: a floating note card on the paper
   surface, with a tinted icon tile, a title, a sentence and one link.

Two rules worth keeping: the card overlaps the photograph but never leaves the
field, and the wordmark is dropped below 960px where it would collide with the
photograph. The card becomes a normal block under the image at that width.



### Anti-references

- **The previous version of this app.** A dark card (`#181b20`) with a 28px
  radius, a vertical gradient fading the dish photo into the card body, frosted
  `backdrop-filter: blur(12px)` badges on top of the image, and pill buttons.
  Every one of those is a recognisable template move.
- **A delivery marketplace.** Neon accents, dark mode by default, glowing
  badges, countdown pressure.
- **A "rustic artisan" brand.** Cream paper, a high-contrast display serif, and
  the word "handcrafted".
- **Brutalism for its own sake.** Sharp corners are a consequence of the
  printed-menu metaphor, not a style in search of a justification.

### What makes an interface read as AI-generated

The problem is never ugliness — it is *undifferentiation*. AI output is the
statistical average of everything in its training data, so it lands on the same
safe choices every time. The specific tells:

1. The default font stack — here, a display serif paired with a geometric sans,
   which is the standard "make it look premium" reflex.
2. Frosted glass over a photograph.
3. Emoji standing in for icons.
4. Every container being the same rounded, softly shadowed card.
5. A hero stat block — four big numbers in a grid.
6. An uppercase eyebrow above every single section heading.
7. The identical rhythm repeated down the page.
8. Copy with no point of view — true, grammatical, and interchangeable.

The fix is not to add more. It is to make specific choices and let them
constrain everything else.

## Design Principles

1. **Every colour must earn its place.** Paprika appears where the user acts and
   where something needs attention. Nowhere else.
2. **Rules, not boxes.** A hairline separates without adding weight. A card
   around every dish is a decision not to decide.
3. **The food is the decoration.** Photography is the one place this interface
   is allowed to be loud. Everything else stays quiet so it can be.
4. **Prices are set in mono.** Anything a user compares — prices, quantities,
   order numbers, delivery times — aligns in tabular figures so it reads as a
   column.
5. **Never cover the dish.** No gradient scrims, no frosted overlays, no text
   sitting on top of the photograph.
6. **State is stated, never implied.** Never rely on colour alone to carry
   meaning. Every status has a word next to it.
7. **Accessibility is structural.** 44px targets, 4.5:1 body contrast, visible
   focus, real buttons and forms. Not a checklist applied at the end.
